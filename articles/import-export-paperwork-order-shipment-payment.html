Import-export paperwork: connect the order, shipment and payment

Trade paperwork is most useful when it explains a complete transaction. Connect the documents so that a supplier payment can be understood alongside the purchase, delivery and customer sale.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

In this article

  3. Record countries and currencies accurately

Start with the commercial roles

Identify who buys, sells, invoices and transports the goods. Explain whether the company is a reseller, intermediary or another participant. The role determines which evidence should exist.

Use a shared transaction reference

Connect quotations and purchase orders to invoices and payment evidence. Add shipping or delivery documents where relevant. A consistent reference makes later questions easier to answer without sending an entire archive.

Record countries and currencies accurately

Describe the actual origin, destination and payment counterparties. Do not replace the real route with Hong Kong simply because the company is incorporated there. Check applicable trade and product requirements for the transaction.

Keep exception explanations

If there is a deposit, partial shipment, refund or payment by another party, document the reason. Unusual transactions should be explained with genuine evidence rather than hidden by a simplified diagram.

A trade transaction worksheet

Role
Buyer, reseller, agent or other genuine role.
Counterparties
Who supplies, buys, invoices and pays.
Route
Actual movement of goods and actual payment route.
Evidence
Agreement, order, invoice and available delivery records.

Work through one real or clearly labelled planned transaction before describing the entire business. Note any deposits, intermediaries or split shipments. The worksheet helps explain the model but does not establish customs compliance or permission to trade a particular product. Check those requirements for the actual goods and jurisdictions rather than assuming company registration answers them.

Frequently asked questions

Are shipping records always the same for every trade?

No. The records depend on the goods, route and commercial arrangement.

Does incorporation remove customs requirements?

No. Relevant customs, product and other requirements still need to be checked.
