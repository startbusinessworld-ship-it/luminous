International business · Practical guide

Planning Asia-facing business operations from Hong Kong

Being closer to Asian markets is an operational goal, not a legal shortcut. Define what the company will do with regional suppliers, customers and partners before deciding the setup.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

In this article

  2. Trace the commercial route

List the regional relationships

Identify existing and intended counterparties by role and location. Separate a supplier from an agent, distributor or customer. Different relationships need different explanations and agreements.

Trace the commercial route

Describe where goods move or services are delivered and which entity invoices. Do not assume a Hong Kong company makes mainland operations automatic. Mainland registration, permissions and other requirements are separate questions.

Plan communication and records

Agree who receives supplier documents and who checks purchase, shipment and payment references. Keep the business explanation understandable to someone who has not followed the entire conversation.

Compare support with the actual need

Company formation, banking preparation and administration solve different problems. Ask for the appropriate task rather than treating a company certificate as a complete regional operating platform.

A cross-border operating map

Value
What the company supplies and where the work occurs.
Contracting
Who signs, invoices and delivers.
Transactions
Customer and supplier payment patterns.
Support
Local company functions and record responsibilities.

Use the map to explain a real business rather than to imply a particular legal or tax result. A customer country, incorporation address and shipment route describe different aspects of the operation. Keep them separate and accurately documented. Ask for specific advice where the proposed activity crosses regulatory or jurisdictional boundaries; a general company guide cannot resolve every case.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Hong Kong company automatically authorise mainland operations?

No. Check the actual mainland activity and applicable local requirements separately.

Can international customers be part of the same model?

Yes, but describe the full transaction pattern and the jurisdictions involved accurately.
