A Hong Kong company is not a work or residence visa

Registering a company does not itself give its founder permission to enter, stay or work in Hong Kong.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 19-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

Registering a company does not itself give its founder permission to enter, stay or work in Hong Kong. Company formation and immigration permission address different matters. Check the rules relevant to the person and planned activity before moving or working locally.

Keep the distinction clear

Company registration is separate from permission to enter, stay or work in Hong Kong. Review the immigration rules relevant to your nationality, circumstances and intended personal activity. Hong Kong Immigration Department: services.

A practical example

An overseas director may be able to hold the company role without residing in Hong Kong. That fact should not be turned into an assurance that the same person can take up local work without considering immigration requirements.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Separate the company role from the proposed personal activity.
  2. Review entry and stay requirements for your circumstances.
  3. Obtain appropriate guidance before relocating or working locally.

Does incorporation automatically include permission to live in Hong Kong?

No. The company's incorporation documents are not a grant of immigration permission. The relevant entry, stay and work rules must be assessed separately.
