Import-export records: connect orders, invoices and payments

Trade records should explain the complete transaction, not just the final movement of money.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 19-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

Trade records should explain the complete transaction, not just the final movement of money. Connect the customer or supplier order with invoices, payment evidence and relevant delivery records. This makes the commercial purpose easier to understand for administration, accounting and account-related questions.

Keep the distinction clear

Business records explain income, expenditure and the commercial activity behind transactions. The Inland Revenue Department describes the record-keeping and retention requirements, including a general minimum of seven years. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department: record keeping.

A practical example

A payment covering several supplier invoices should remain traceable to those invoices. A simple reconciliation note can be more useful than leaving the transfer as an unexplained amount in the bank statement.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Use transaction or order references consistently.
  2. Link settlement evidence to the relevant invoices.
  3. Retain supporting delivery and commercial records.

Does a bank transfer prove the complete purpose of a trade transaction?

Not on its own. Keep the documents that explain what the payment was for and the relationship behind it.
