A Hong Kong company serving overseas clients

For a service business, the company should match the way client relationships are documented.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 14-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

For a service business, the company should match the way client relationships are documented. Contracts, invoices and payment records should clearly identify the entity supplying the service. International customers alone do not determine the company's tax treatment or remove obligations in other jurisdictions.

Keep the distinction clear

A company structure, payment arrangements and the rules in the countries where a business operates are separate considerations. Plan them together without assuming that incorporation settles every cross-border question. Hong Kong Companies Registry: incorporation.

A practical example

A consultant billing clients abroad should retain the engagement, scope of work and supporting payment records. Those records explain the business more effectively than a broad label such as global services.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Use consistent company details on client documents.
  2. Describe the services and delivery arrangements.
  3. Consider obligations where the work is actually performed.

Are foreign clients enough to make all profits tax-exempt?

No. Tax treatment depends on the relevant rules and facts, including the profit-generating operations. It should not be inferred from customer location alone.
