Working with a company support team: contacts, tasks and follow-up

A team photograph shows the people behind a service. A useful working relationship goes further: you should know who handles the request, what information is needed and how progress is confirmed.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Give the team one coherent brief

State the business activity, current stage and support needed. If several founders are involved, agree the information before submitting it. Contradictory descriptions create unnecessary clarification rounds.

Assign responsibility on your side

Choose who collects documents and who can approve decisions. Keep other stakeholders informed without creating competing instructions. A single action log makes the handover easier when a person is unavailable.

Keep questions attached to evidence

When answering a request, refer to the exact question and relevant document. Explain missing items instead of silently leaving them out. Keep commercially sensitive information limited to the appropriate secure channel.

Confirm what has actually happened

Distinguish a draft, a submitted file and a completed registration. For accounts, distinguish preparation from a provider decision. Ask for the confirmation relevant to that stage rather than relying on a general progress message.

A useful follow-up record

Request
Write the precise task or question.
Input
List the information genuinely available.
Responsibility
Identify who supplies the answer or document.
Next status
Record what will confirm completion of this stage.

Use this record after a conversation, especially when several founders or advisers are involved. Keep one current version and attach important decisions. If the next outcome depends on a provider or authority, record that dependency instead of replacing it with an assumed completion date. Do not place passwords or private identification in a general action log.

Frequently asked questions

Does the team replace my responsibility to provide accurate information?

No. The business must provide genuine and complete information about its activities and participants.
