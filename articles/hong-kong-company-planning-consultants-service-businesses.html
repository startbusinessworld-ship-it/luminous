Hong Kong company planning for consultants and service businesses

A service business may have no container or warehouse, but it still needs a clear commercial record. Explain what you deliver, who contracts with you and how completion and payment are documented.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Define the service precisely

Replace consulting with a description of the actual work, such as design deliverables or a defined advisory project. Identify the customer type and delivery method. Avoid implying qualifications or permissions the business does not hold.

Make contract and invoice descriptions agree

Use a clear scope, delivery milestones and payment terms. Keep customer acceptance or delivery evidence where available. An invoice should relate to an identifiable service rather than an unexplained transfer.

Explain overseas delivery honestly

Describe where the people doing the work are located and where management decisions occur. Overseas customers alone do not determine a company's tax treatment. Seek advice based on how the work is actually carried out.

Prepare the account narrative

Show how clients find the business, the expected payment pattern and the evidence available. For a new venture, distinguish proposals from signed contracts and projections from historical turnover.

A cross-border operating map

Value
What the company supplies and where the work occurs.
Contracting
Who signs, invoices and delivers.
Transactions
Customer and supplier payment patterns.
Support
Local company functions and record responsibilities.

Use the map to explain a real business rather than to imply a particular legal or tax result. A customer country, incorporation address and shipment route describe different aspects of the operation. Keep them separate and accurately documented. Ask for specific advice where the proposed activity crosses regulatory or jurisdictional boundaries; a general company guide cannot resolve every case.

Frequently asked questions

Can a service business use a Hong Kong company?

The suitability depends on the activity and operating circumstances; define those before choosing the structure.

What replaces shipping evidence for services?

Relevant agreements, delivery records, project correspondence or acceptance evidence can explain the transaction.
