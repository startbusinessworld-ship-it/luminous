Business records: what the seven-year rule means

Hong Kong's record-keeping rules require sufficient business records to establish income, expenditure and assessable profits, with retention generally for at least seven years.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 15-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

Hong Kong's record-keeping rules require sufficient business records to establish income, expenditure and assessable profits, with retention generally for at least seven years. Records include more than invoices: bank statements, receipts and information about assets and liabilities can also be relevant.

Keep the distinction clear

Business records explain income, expenditure and the commercial activity behind transactions. The Inland Revenue Department describes the record-keeping and retention requirements, including a general minimum of seven years. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department: record keeping.

A practical example

For a goods purchase, retain the invoice alongside the order, payment and relevant delivery evidence. This helps explain the transaction later instead of rebuilding its commercial story from a single bank entry.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Keep transaction evidence as activity happens.
  2. Link invoices, payments and supporting records.
  3. Maintain a retention and retrieval process.

Is a folder containing only invoices enough?

Not necessarily. Records must be sufficient for the business and the relevant rules. Keep the supporting evidence behind the amounts, not just the invoice totals.
