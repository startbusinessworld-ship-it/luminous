Turning a business vision into a workable company setup brief

A vision describes where you want to go. A setup brief explains how the business will actually operate. Connecting the two helps keep the formation process grounded in real activity.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Translate the idea into a sale

Describe one typical customer order: what is purchased, how it is delivered and how the company gets paid. If there are several revenue streams, describe each separately.

Identify the operational dependencies

List suppliers, sales channels, premises if relevant and service providers. Mark whether each relationship exists or is only being explored. This prevents plans being mistaken for signed arrangements.

Describe the people behind the project

Explain who owns the business and who will manage it. Identify the person responsible for providing information and responding to questions. Keep identity information accurate and shared securely.

Choose the first questions to resolve

Ask whether the intended structure, application documents and administration arrangements fit the model. Do not let a logo, website or certificate stand in for an operating plan.

What must be resolved before proceeding?

For an early-stage business, complete the worksheet with confirmed facts first. Leave an unknown marked as unknown and assign a way to resolve it. For an existing business, use actual contracts and transactions to test whether the current description is still accurate. The worksheet is a discussion aid, not a determination of legal or tax status.

Frequently asked questions

How detailed should the brief be?

Enough to explain a typical transaction, the people involved and the remaining uncertainties without adding unsupported claims.

Can I update the brief later?

Yes. Keep dated versions and explain significant changes when they affect an application or existing arrangement.
