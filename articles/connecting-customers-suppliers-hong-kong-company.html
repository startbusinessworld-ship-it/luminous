Connecting customers and suppliers through a Hong Kong company

A connected business has a traceable chain from customer order to supplier payment. Company formation provides a legal starting point; the commercial chain still needs to be explained.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Draw one transaction from beginning to end

Show who places an order, who supplies the goods or service, who invoices and who receives payment. Include the countries involved without assuming every shipment passes through Hong Kong.

Identify the company's role

State whether the company buys and resells goods, acts as an agent or provides services. Those models produce different contracts and records. Avoid using the word trading as the entire activity description.

Keep the supporting links

Link quotations to orders, orders to invoices and payments to the relevant counterparty. For goods, keep available shipping evidence with the same reference. A reviewer should not have to guess which records belong together.

Discuss the missing pieces

Tell Luminos which element is still being prepared: entity, company file, account application or ongoing administration. Distinguishing the tasks makes the next step concrete.

A cross-border operating map

Use the map to explain a real business rather than to imply a particular legal or tax result. A customer country, incorporation address and shipment route describe different aspects of the operation. Keep them separate and accurately documented. Ask for specific advice where the proposed activity crosses regulatory or jurisdictional boundaries; a general company guide cannot resolve every case.

Frequently asked questions

Must goods physically enter Hong Kong?

Not necessarily; describe the actual route and check the rules relevant to the goods and countries involved.

Is being incorporated enough to start every activity?

No. Some activities or transactions need separate permissions or checks.
