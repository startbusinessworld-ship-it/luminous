The company behind your online store

An online store presents a brand, but a legal entity carries out its transactions.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 15-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

An online store presents a brand, but a legal entity carries out its transactions. Decide which company appears on contracts, invoices and payment-provider applications. Those details should reflect the business actually operating the store, rather than changing from one document to the next.

Keep the distinction clear

The company and storefront should describe a consistent business. Payment-product access, record keeping and rules relevant to the markets where you operate need separate attention. Hong Kong Monetary Authority: information for account opening.

A practical example

A merchant preparing an account application can explain its storefront, products, suppliers and customer markets together. Consistent company details make the activity easier to understand; they do not guarantee provider acceptance.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Identify the legal entity operating the storefront.
  2. Match store and supplier records to that entity.
  3. Check payment-product eligibility for the activity.

Will creating a Hong Kong company automatically activate my checkout?

No. A payment processor may require a separate review and may apply product, location and business-activity restrictions.
