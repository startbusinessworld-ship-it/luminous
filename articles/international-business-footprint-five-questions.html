Building an international business footprint: five practical questions

An international footprint should be understandable to customers, suppliers and service providers. The five-slide visual is a starting point for examining how the business will function across borders.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

In this article

  1. Where does the business create value?

  3. Which records connect the footprint?
  4. What support is needed locally?
  5. A cross-border operating map
  6. Frequently asked questions

Where does the business create value?

Explain the product or service and where the work is performed. Distinguish customer locations from management and delivery locations. A map of markets alone does not describe the operating model.

Who contracts and who gets paid?

Identify the contracting entity on each side of a transaction. Keep website terms, invoices and payment arrangements consistent with that entity. Clarify agency or reseller relationships rather than leaving the role ambiguous.

Which records connect the footprint?

Keep orders, agreements, invoices and payment evidence linked by reference. Add relevant shipment or delivery records. This helps explain the business without relying on a polished presentation alone.

What support is needed locally?

Identify correspondence, company administration and recordkeeping responsibilities. Then distinguish those services from banking or activity-specific approvals. Choose the next step based on an actual gap in the operating model.

A cross-border operating map

Use the map to explain a real business rather than to imply a particular legal or tax result. A customer country, incorporation address and shipment route describe different aspects of the operation. Keep them separate and accurately documented. Ask for specific advice where the proposed activity crosses regulatory or jurisdictional boundaries; a general company guide cannot resolve every case.

Frequently asked questions

Does an overseas customer automatically make income offshore?

No. A customer's address alone does not determine the tax treatment of profits.

Should all functions be in the same place?

Not necessarily, but their real locations and responsibilities need to be understood and assessed.
