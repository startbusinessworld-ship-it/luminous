A company structure for e-commerce and import-export

A checkout page is only one part of a trading business.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 19-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

A checkout page is only one part of a trading business. The company behind it enters contracts, buys goods, issues invoices and keeps records. A Hong Kong company may provide a structure for those activities, but its usefulness depends on the real business model and the obligations that follow.

Keep the distinction clear

A company structure, payment arrangements and the rules in the countries where a business operates are separate considerations. Plan them together without assuming that incorporation settles every cross-border question. Hong Kong Companies Registry: incorporation.

A practical example

A seller buying stock overseas should be able to connect supplier invoices, company purchases, customer orders and payments. The original office footage in the reel illustrates practical administration, not guaranteed earnings or a promised banking result.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Identify the entity that contracts with customers and suppliers.
  2. Keep the trading records under that entity.
  3. Plan administration alongside your payment application.

Does forming a company replace the work of running the store?

No. Incorporation creates the entity. Product operations, payment access, records and ongoing obligations still need to be managed.
