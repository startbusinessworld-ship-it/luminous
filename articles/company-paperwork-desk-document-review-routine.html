Sort by purpose before checking

Separate company identity records, commercial evidence and correspondence. Review one category at a time so an unrelated document does not conceal a missing item.

Check names, dates and versions

Compare details across the relevant records. Flag inconsistencies and ask what needs correction rather than silently editing an official copy.

Keep a secure review trail

Record the issue, response and final version. Use redacted copies for public presentation and restrict access to the working files that contain personal information.

A transaction evidence index

Reference
Order, project or invoice identifier.
Commercial reason
The underlying goods, services or agreement.
Payment evidence
The relevant receipt, statement entry or transfer.
Exception
Explanation for a refund, split payment or other difference.

The index should point to genuine source records. It is not a substitute for the invoice, contract or statement itself. Review gaps regularly and keep a record of how they were resolved. If the same transaction involves several currencies or partial deliveries, retain the explanation rather than changing the original evidence to make the figures appear identical.

Frequently asked questions

Does an index replace the original documents?

No. Keep the underlying records; the index helps you locate and connect them.

When should I deal with missing information?

Review gaps regularly while the people and transaction details are still accessible.
