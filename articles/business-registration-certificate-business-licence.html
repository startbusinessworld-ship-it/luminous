Business registration is not a business licence

A Business Registration Certificate does not authorise every possible activity.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 19-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

A Business Registration Certificate does not authorise every possible activity. Hong Kong businesses may need additional licences, approvals or registrations depending on what they actually do. Start from the proposed activity rather than interpreting the certificate as a universal trading permission.

Keep the distinction clear

Business registration does not replace any additional permission that applies to a particular activity. Check the relevant licensing requirements before relying on a certificate as authority to trade. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department: business registration.

A practical example

A founder changing from ordinary consulting to an activity with specific regulatory requirements should check the new activity before offering it. The fact that the company already exists does not settle the licensing question.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Describe the activity precisely.
  2. Check activity-specific permissions before trading.
  3. Recheck requirements when the business model changes.

Does an existing company certificate cover any new activity?

No. Company registration, business registration and activity-specific permissions are distinct. Check the requirements relevant to the new activity.
