Clearer business records: matching invoices, statements and contracts

Good records let you explain a transaction without reconstructing it from memory. Connect the commercial agreement, invoice and payment evidence while the details are still easy to verify.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

In this article

  2. Match bank activity to the business
  3. Review missing information regularly

Choose an organising reference

Use an order, project or invoice reference consistently across the records. Save related documents together or link them in an index. Keep the original document as well as any working summary.

Match bank activity to the business

Reconcile receipts and payments with the relevant invoices or explanations. Identify fees, refunds and partial payments rather than forcing a false one-to-one match. Keep a note of genuine exceptions.

Review missing information regularly

Check for missing statements, unexplained transfers and incomplete counterparty details. Ask questions early. The person who handled the transaction may not remember the detail months later.

Store records securely and for the required period

The Hong Kong IRD describes business recordkeeping obligations, generally including retention for at least seven years. Confirm how the rules apply to your records and preserve access if a provider or staff member changes.

A transaction evidence index

Reference
Order, project or invoice identifier.
Commercial reason
The underlying goods, services or agreement.
Payment evidence
The relevant receipt, statement entry or transfer.
Exception
Explanation for a refund, split payment or other difference.

The index should point to genuine source records. It is not a substitute for the invoice, contract or statement itself. Review gaps regularly and keep a record of how they were resolved. If the same transaction involves several currencies or partial deliveries, retain the explanation rather than changing the original evidence to make the figures appear identical.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bank statement a substitute for every invoice?

No. A statement shows a payment but may not explain the underlying supply or agreement.

Can I use a spreadsheet as an index?

Yes, but keep the supporting source records and control access; an index alone does not prove a transaction.
