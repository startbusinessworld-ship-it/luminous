Explain ownership, activity and payments in a bank file

A bank needs to understand who is behind the company, what it does and the intended account use.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 18-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

A bank needs to understand who is behind the company, what it does and the intended account use. Keep these explanations connected. The ownership structure, the products or services and expected transactions should describe one consistent business rather than three unrelated answers.

Keep the distinction clear

A financial institution reviews the business and its intended account use. Supporting documents should be genuine, relevant and consistent; no formation service can guarantee the institution’s approval. Hong Kong Monetary Authority: information for account opening.

A practical example

A trader expecting supplier payments should explain the supplier relationships supporting that activity. If forecasts are provisional, identify them as estimates instead of presenting planned transactions as completed sales.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. Explain ownership and control accurately.
  2. Describe the real business activity.
  3. Support expected payment flows with available records.

Should I inflate projected activity to make the application look stronger?

No. Provide accurate information and identify estimates honestly. Misleading statements do not replace a genuine explanation of the business.
