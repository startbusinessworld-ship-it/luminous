Business accounts · Practical guide

Preparing a Hong Kong business account application after incorporation

A company certificate is a starting record, not an account approval. Prepare an application that explains the owners, real business activity and expected transactions clearly.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Identify the service you need

Separate receiving and sending payments, currency balances, payment acceptance and invoicing. Not every provider offers every feature to every business. Confirm the available product for the company's actual circumstances.

Read the recipient's checklist

Requirements vary by provider and business profile. Company and participant information may be needed alongside commercial evidence. Avoid treating a generic list from a social post as the complete official application checklist.

Explain the expected flows

Describe customers, suppliers, countries and currencies. Use genuine contracts or other available evidence. Label a new business as new and distinguish estimates from established transaction history.

Prepare for follow-up questions

Keep a copy of the submitted information and respond consistently to requests. Explain changes or missing evidence directly. Assistance with preparation does not control the provider's decision.

An application preparation worksheet

Business model
Goods or services, customers and sales channels.
Ownership
Participants and any ownership chain.
Payments
Expected counterparties, countries and currencies.
Evidence
Genuine records supporting the description.

Use the worksheet to organise a conversation, then follow the selected provider’s current requirements. It is not a universal document list. A new venture should identify forecasts and pre-launch evidence honestly. An existing business should explain its actual activity and any changes. Completing this preparation does not guarantee acceptance, a particular review time or access to every product.

Frequently asked questions

Is opening an account automatic after registration?

No. Each provider conducts its own review and decides whether to offer the service.

Are all online account providers banks?

No. Legal status, safeguards and product terms vary; check the specific provider and jurisdiction.
