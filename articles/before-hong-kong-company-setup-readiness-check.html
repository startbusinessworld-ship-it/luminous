Before setting up a Hong Kong company: an entrepreneur's readiness check

Readiness does not mean having every answer. It means knowing what the business will do, what information is genuine and which decisions still need to be made.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Write a one-page business description

Include the offer, customer type, sales channel and delivery method. Explain whether you already trade. If you are testing an idea, label it as a plan rather than an operating history.

Prepare the participant information

Identify proposed owners and directors and the role each will have. Ask how to provide identification securely. Clarify an ownership chain before sending disconnected documents.

Plan the support functions

Discuss company secretary and registered office arrangements, document access and ongoing administration. Make sure you understand what is included in the proposed service and what happens after registration.

Keep the next steps separate

Company registration, commercial launch and account opening have different requirements. Build a sequence with those distinctions so an early milestone is not mistaken for full operational readiness.

A formation preparation record

Proposed details
Activity, intended name and company type.
Participants
Proposed owners, directors and their roles.
Local arrangements
Required company functions and correspondence.
Stage
Draft, ready for review, submitted or registered.

Keep proposals separate from the information actually submitted. If a detail changes, confirm which parts of the file need updating and preserve the earlier version where relevant. Use current official forms for the actual company type. This preparation record is not an incorporation form and does not replace the company’s issued documents after successful registration.

Frequently asked questions

Must I have customers already?

Not necessarily, but describe the actual stage honestly and provide genuine planning evidence where requested.

Can I assume a business account comes with incorporation?

No. Prepare for a separate provider review.
