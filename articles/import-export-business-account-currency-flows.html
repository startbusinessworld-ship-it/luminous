Match an import-export account to your trade flows

An import-export account should be assessed against actual transactions.

By Luminos · 12 November 2026 · 19-second video

Read the practical guide ↓

The practical answer

An import-export account should be assessed against actual transactions. Look at how customers pay, the currencies on supplier invoices and the routes through which funds will move. Compare an institution's supported services with those needs instead of assuming every international account works the same way.

Keep the distinction clear

A financial institution reviews the business and its intended account use. Supporting documents should be genuine, relevant and consistent; no formation service can guarantee the institution’s approval. Hong Kong Monetary Authority: information for account opening.

A practical example

A trader can receive a customer order in one country and purchase goods from a supplier in another. Keeping the order, invoice and payment evidence connected makes the transaction easier to explain during an account review.

This is an illustration of the process, not a promised outcome for an individual application.

What to prepare next

  1. List expected incoming and outgoing currencies.
  2. Explain the commercial reason for each payment route.
  3. Keep orders, invoices and settlement records together.

Will every account support all my supplier countries?

No. Availability depends on the provider, country, business activity and product terms. Confirm the relevant routes before relying on an account.
