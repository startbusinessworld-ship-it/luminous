Hong Kong company ownership: record who owns and controls the business

Ownership is more than a name on a presentation. A useful company file explains who holds shares, who makes decisions and how the ownership information is kept current.

By Luminos editorial team · 13 November 2026

Distinguish the roles

A shareholder owns shares; a director manages company affairs. One person may hold more than one role, but the records should still distinguish them. Company secretary and registered office arrangements are separate functions.

Make the ownership chain readable

If a shareholder is another company, prepare a clear ownership chart and relevant supporting records. Identify the people behind the structure accurately instead of stopping at the first corporate shareholder.

Record changes rather than overwriting history

Keep dated records of ownership changes and ask what formal steps apply before treating a proposed change as completed. A changed spreadsheet is not a substitute for the required company documentation.

Explain the commercial reason

If there are several owners or entities, say why the arrangement exists and who will communicate with advisers and providers. Straightforward explanations are more useful than unnecessary complexity.

Use the map to distinguish roles even when one person holds several of them. Keep supporting records consistent with the explanation. If an ownership or appointment change is proposed, ask what formal steps apply before treating it as complete. A presentation chart helps understanding but does not itself amend the company’s official records.

Frequently asked questions

Can an owner also be a director?

Yes, the roles may be held by the same person, subject to the applicable company requirements.

Does ownership alone give permission to work in Hong Kong?

No. Immigration and company ownership are separate matters.
